Before the sign goes in the yard

Preparation should be strategic, not expensive.

There is a difference between making a home market-ready and spending money simply because you are about to sell. The goal is to remove avoidable objections, make the property easier to appreciate and focus your effort where buyers are most likely to notice.

This guide gives you a clear starting point. Complete the practical items you can, then talk with me before committing to major improvements.

The 8-point preparation checklist

Focus on the areas buyers notice first.

A well-prepared home photographs better, shows better and can help buyers feel more confident about the property.

01 · CLEAN THOROUGHLY

Make care visible.

  • Deep clean kitchens and baths
  • Clean floors, carpets, windows and mirrors
  • Dust vents, fans, trim and baseboards
  • Address pet hair and noticeable odors
02 · MAKE SMALL REPAIRS

Remove easy objections.

  • Fix obvious leaks and loose hardware
  • Patch unnecessary wall holes
  • Replace burned-out bulbs
  • Check doors, windows and toilets
03 · TOUCH UP PAINT

Freshen what buyers see.

  • Address scuffed or damaged areas
  • Use neutral, consistent finishes
  • Focus on high-visibility rooms first
04 · IMPROVE LIGHTING

Make rooms feel brighter.

  • Replace failed bulbs
  • Use consistent bulb temperatures
  • Open blinds and curtains where appropriate
05 · BOOST CURB APPEAL

Earn the first impression.

  • Mow, trim and edge
  • Remove weeds, leaves and debris
  • Clean the front entrance
  • Check exterior lighting and house numbers
06 · DECLUTTER & DEPERSONALIZE

Create breathing room.

  • Clear counters and surfaces
  • Reduce excess furniture
  • Organize closets and storage
  • Pack seasonal and rarely used items
07 · PREPARE FOR PHOTOS

Let the home tell the story.

  • Make beds and put away laundry
  • Minimize cords and miscellaneous clutter
  • Remove pet items from primary photo areas
  • Remove vehicles from the driveway when possible
08 · GATHER IMPORTANT DOCUMENTS

Be ready with the facts.

  • Warranties and manuals
  • Utility information
  • Repair and improvement records
  • Roof, HVAC and water-heater information
“Small details create big impressions.”

Buyers notice how a home is cared for. Preparation shows pride of ownership and helps reduce uncertainty.
